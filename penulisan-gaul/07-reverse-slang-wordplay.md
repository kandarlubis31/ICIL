# 📝 07 — Reverse Slang & Wordplay

> 🔴 Advanced | Prereq: 01, 03 | ~10 min

Indonesian informal writing has a rich tradition of linguistic play — reversing syllables, shifting sounds, and creating secret codes. From Malang's *boso walikan* to Jakarta's *bahasa kebalikan*, wordplay is both creative expression and in-group gatekeeping.

---

## 7.1 The Four Types of Indonesian Wordplay

### 1. Syllable Reversal (Boso Walikan / Bahasa Kebalikan)

| Original | Reversed | Region |
|----------|----------|--------|
| *Malang* | *Ngalam* | Malang, East Java |
| *Bisa* | *Sabi* | National (gaming → general) |
| *Makan* | *Kanma* | Playful/fleeting |
| *Kamu* | *Umak* | Affectionate/teasing |

**Rule**: Reverse the syllable order. For 2-syllable words: swap A-B → B-A. For multi-syllable: usually reverse the last two syllables.

### 2. Sound Substitution

| Pattern | Example | Region |
|---------|---------|--------|
| B → P, D → T | *Bandung* → *Pan-dung* | Sundanese-influenced |
| Final -ng → -k | *Kurang* → *Kurak* | Jakarta street |
| -a → -e (Javanese) | *Saya* → *Saye* | Java-wide |

### 3. Insertion Play (Bahasa G)

Insert *-g-* + vowel after each syllable:
- *Aku* → *A-gu-ku-gu*
- *Makan* → *Ma-ga-kan-gan*

Mostly child's play, but occasionally used in ironic adult contexts.

### 4. Letter Substitution (Leet/Alay Script)

| Letter | Replacement |
|--------|------------|
| a | 4 |
| e | 3 |
| i | 1 |
| o | 0 |
| s | 5 |
| g | 9 |

Example: *s4y4 m4k4n* = *saya makan*. This Alay-era encoding is now mostly ironic/nostalgic.

## 7.2 The Sabi Phenomenon: From Gaming to Mainstream

*Sabi* (← *bisa*) is the most successful wordplay term in modern Indonesian. Its journey:

```
2020: Gaming circles
  "Nanti malam mabar sabi?"
2021: General youth
  "Sabi lah, gue ikut!"
2022: Brand adoption
  "SABI banget promo kita!" 
2023: Mainstream
  Used in TV ads, billboards, political campaigns
```

### Writer's Rule for Reversal Slang

| Context | Use Reversal? | Strategy |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| Gaming/tech audience | ✅ Yes | *Sabi* is standard vocabulary |
| Youth brand (Gen Z) | ✅ Yes | Signals insider knowledge |
| Mass market (30+) | ⚠️ Test first | May not land |
| Formal/professional | ❌ No | Too casual |

## 7.3 Creative Wordplay Techniques for Writers

| Technique | Example | Effect |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Blended reversal** | *Gaskeun* (gas + Sundanese *-keun*) | Regional flavor |
| **Rhyming reduplication** | *Bolak-balik, mondar-mandir* | Playful rhythm |
| **Sound symbolism** | *Ciee...* (teasing), *Duh* (frustration) | Emotional punctuation |
| **Onomatopoeic play** | *Jedag-jedug* (EDM beat) | Sensory writing |

## 7.4 Regional Wordplay Traditions

| Region | Tradition | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Malang** | Boso Walikan | *Ngalam, Oyi Sam* |
| **Jakarta** | Prokem suffixes | *-ok-* insertion: *bokap, nyokap* |
| **Sunda** | *-keun, -eun* suffix play | *Gaskeun, atuh* |
| **Medan** | *-lah, -pun* emphasis | *Apalah, iyapun* |
| **Manado** | *Ngana, ngoni* pronouns | "Ngana so pigi?" |
